Car crashes are common in Utah. In 2023 the state recorded **60,021 crashes** and **26,637 injuries** [[1]](#source-1). One of the most frequent injuries is whiplash: a strain of the neck caused by a sudden back-and-forth movement, often in a rear-end collision.

Here is what the research says about recovery, what physical therapy guidelines recommend, and how Utah’s auto insurance pays for care.

## How whiplash is graded

Clinicians often use the Quebec Task Force grades to describe how serious a whiplash injury is [[2]](#source-2):

| Grade | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No neck complaints and no physical signs |
| I | Neck pain, stiffness or tenderness only, with no physical signs |
| II | Neck complaints plus musculoskeletal signs, such as reduced range of motion or tender points |
| III | Neck complaints plus neurological signs, such as weak or absent reflexes, weakness or loss of sensation |
| IV | Neck complaints plus a fracture or dislocation |

*Source: Quebec Task Force classification, as reproduced by Shergill et al., 2021 [[2]](#source-2).*

Higher grades tend to have a slower recovery [[2]](#source-2). **Numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms, or severe neck pain after a crash, need a medical evaluation right away**, because they can point to a nerve injury or a fracture (grades III and IV).

## What recovery usually looks like

Most of the improvement happens early. The physical therapy clinical practice guideline for neck pain summarizes research showing that people with whiplash tend to follow one of three paths, and that recovery is fastest in the first 6 to 12 weeks, slows considerably after that, and is small after 12 months [[3]](#source-3):

A large review by an international task force found that **about half of people with whiplash still report neck pain a year after the injury** [[4]](#source-4). Recovery was slower for people with more pain, more symptoms and more disability at the start. Details of the crash itself, like the direction of impact or the type of headrest, predicted little. Factors such as fear of movement, low mood and a passive way of coping did [[4]](#source-4). The neck pain guideline adds post-traumatic stress symptoms and strong catastrophic beliefs to the list of warning signs for a slower recovery [[3]](#source-3).

That is good news in one way: several of these factors can be addressed with the right education and care.

## What physical therapy guidelines recommend

The American Physical Therapy Association’s neck pain guideline gives clear direction for whiplash [[3]](#source-3):

**In the first weeks (acute phase)**

- Education to **return to normal, non-provocative activities as soon as possible**
- **Minimal use of a cervical collar**
- Posture and mobility exercises to reduce pain and restore range of motion
- Reassurance that recovery is expected within the first 2 to 3 months

**If recovery is expected to be slower**

- A combined approach of hands-on mobilization plus exercise: strengthening, endurance, flexibility, posture, coordination, aerobic and functional exercises
- Regular monitoring to catch delayed recovery early, so that more intensive rehabilitation and pain education can start in time

**If symptoms become chronic**

- Education focused on reassurance, encouragement, prognosis and pain management
- Mobilization combined with an individualized, progressive exercise program

One point from the guideline is worth highlighting: **more treatment is not automatically better**. For people at low risk of long-term problems, the guideline allows a single session of advice, exercise instruction and education. In one study it cites, more people who received intensive physical therapy for acute whiplash still reported symptoms two years later than people who received one session of home exercises and advice [[3]](#source-3). A good first visit therefore includes a screening of your risk factors, so your plan matches your situation.

## How Utah PIP pays for care

Utah is a no-fault auto insurance state. Every Utah auto policy must include personal injury protection (PIP), with a few exceptions such as motorcycles and off-highway vehicles [[5]](#source-5). The Utah Insurance Department describes PIP as no-fault coverage [[8]](#source-8). In practice, your own policy pays your PIP benefits, and your insurer can later seek reimbursement from the insurer of the driver at fault [[7]](#source-7).

The minimum PIP benefits required by Utah law are [[6]](#source-6):

| Benefit | Utah minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Medical expenses, including rehabilitation services | At least $3,000 per person |
| Lost income | The lesser of $250 per week or 85% of lost gross income, for up to 52 weeks |
| Household services you cannot do | Up to $20 per day, for up to 365 days |
| Funeral, burial or cremation | Up to $1,500 per person |
| Death benefit | $3,000 |

*Source: Utah Code 31A-22-307 [[6]](#source-6). Your policy may include more than the minimum.*

A few other rules worth knowing:

- **No deductibles** are allowed on the required PIP coverage [[6]](#source-6).
- PIP pays **monthly as expenses come in**. Payment is overdue if the insurer has not paid within 30 days of receiving reasonable proof of the expense [[7]](#source-7).
- The policy on the vehicle involved in the accident is generally the primary coverage [[7]](#source-7).
- In Utah you can sue for general damages (such as pain and suffering) after a crash only if the injury meets a threshold: death, dismemberment, permanent disability or impairment, permanent disfigurement, a broken bone, or **medical expenses over $3,000** [[7]](#source-7).

Insurance and legal questions depend on your specific policy and situation, so talk to your insurer or an attorney about your case.

## The takeaway

- Whiplash ranges from mild neck pain to injuries that need urgent care. Arm numbness, weakness or severe pain should be checked right away.
- Most recovery happens in the first 6 to 12 weeks, but about half of people still have some neck pain a year later.
- Guidelines recommend staying active, limiting the collar, and exercise, with more hands-on care for people at risk of a slow recovery.
- In Utah, the no-fault PIP coverage on your own policy pays at least $3,000 in medical expenses, including rehabilitation.
