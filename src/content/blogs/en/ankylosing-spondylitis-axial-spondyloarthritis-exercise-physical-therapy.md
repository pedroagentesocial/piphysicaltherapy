Most back pain gets better with rest. This kind does the opposite. It gets worse after sitting or lying still for a long time, it can be worse at night, and it eases once you get up and move. If that sounds familiar, and especially if it started when you were young, it may be worth asking about ankylosing spondylitis.

Ankylosing spondylitis is a type of inflammatory arthritis, and it is very different from a strained back. It is treated with medicine, and also with something that may seem counterintuitive for a painful spine: regular exercise. US guidelines strongly recommend physical therapy for it [[6]](#source-6). Here is what the research shows.

## What ankylosing spondylitis is

Ankylosing spondylitis is a form of arthritis that causes inflammation in the joints and ligaments of the spine. It can also affect other joints, such as the hips, knees and ankles [[1]](#source-1). Doctors now often group it under a broader name, axial spondyloarthritis, which also includes people whose inflammation does not yet show up on X-rays. Treatment recommendations for both are similar [[6]](#source-6).

The most common symptom is pain and stiffness in the lower back or hips [[1]](#source-1). What sets it apart is the pattern [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2):

- Pain and stiffness that get worse with rest, so they may be worse at night or after sitting for a long time
- Symptoms that improve with movement and exercise
- Flares, when symptoms worsen, followed by calmer periods
- Fatigue, and sometimes pain or swelling in other joints

It usually starts early. Most people notice symptoms before age 45, and early symptoms typically start between 15 and 30 [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2). Symptoms tend to build gradually over months or years [[3]](#source-3).

## Who gets it

A gene called HLA-B27 raises the risk, but most people who carry it never develop the disease, and some people without it do [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2). A family history also raises the risk, as do Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and psoriasis [[1]](#source-1).

Based on a US national survey of adults aged 20 to 69, spondyloarthritis may affect up to about 1% of American adults, a rate similar to rheumatoid arthritis [[4]](#source-4).

Because back pain is so common, the diagnosis is often slow. In a study of 554 people with axial spondyloarthritis in the Boston area, the median time from the start of back pain to diagnosis was 3.8 years [[5]](#source-5).

## Why it matters

Inflammation often starts in the sacroiliac joints, where the spine meets the pelvis, and can spread up the spine [[2]](#source-2). In severe cases, the bones of the spine fuse, which makes it rigid and less flexible [[1]](#source-1). If the joints where the ribs meet the spine are affected, deep breathing can become hard [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2). Ankylosing spondylitis can also lead to weakened bones, spine fractures and heart disease [[3]](#source-3).

It is not only a joint disease. Eye inflammation, called iritis or uveitis, occurs in more than 30% of people with it and causes eye pain and sensitivity to light [[2]](#source-2). Between 6% and 10% of people with ankylosing spondylitis also have psoriasis or an inflammatory bowel disease such as Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis [[2]](#source-2).

## How it is treated

There is no cure, but treatment can relieve symptoms and help slow the disease, and with treatment most people with ankylosing spondylitis lead productive lives [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3). Care usually combines exercise and physical therapy with medicine [[3]](#source-3).

Anti-inflammatory medicines (NSAIDs) are the first-choice drugs. When the disease stays active after trying at least two of them, and tests or scans confirm inflammation, biologic medicines that target the immune system are the next step [[7]](#source-7). The international guidelines discourage long-term steroid pills for disease of the spine [[7]](#source-7). Surgery is uncommon and is mainly used for badly damaged joints or a severe bend in the spine [[3]](#source-3).

Here is what the main guidelines say about physical therapy and exercise:

| Recommendation | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Physical therapy over no physical therapy (strong recommendation) | American College of Rheumatology and partners, 2019 [[6]](#source-6) |
| Active therapy, meaning supervised exercise, over passive treatments such as massage, ultrasound or heat (conditional recommendation) | American College of Rheumatology and partners, 2019 [[6]](#source-6) |
| Land-based exercise over pool-based therapy (conditional recommendation) | American College of Rheumatology and partners, 2019 [[6]](#source-6) |
| Exercise, physical therapy and occupational therapy to improve mobility and posture | NIH [[1]](#source-1) |

## What exercise trials show

### Exercise compared with no exercise

A 2019 Cochrane review pooled 14 trials with 1,579 people with ankylosing spondylitis. Most programs lasted about 12 weeks, focused on strength, flexibility, stretching and breathing, and were done alongside medicine [[8]](#source-8). The drop in pain was large enough to matter in daily life, though the evidence for it was low quality. The gains in function and disease activity were real but small [[8]](#source-8). When exercise programs were compared with usual care, which often already includes some activity, the extra benefit was small [[8]](#source-8). There was too little data to judge side effects with confidence [[8]](#source-8).

### Supervised group sessions and home exercise

An earlier Cochrane review found that both a home exercise program and a supervised program were better than doing nothing. Supervised group physical therapy did somewhat better than exercising at home, with small gains in spinal mobility and in people's overall rating of their condition [[9]](#source-9).

### Is hard exercise safe?

People with inflammatory arthritis have long worried that intense exercise could stir up the disease. A trial led from Norway tested that directly. It assigned 100 people with axial spondyloarthritis to 3 months of high-intensity cardio and strength training or to usual care [[10]](#source-10). Ninety-seven completed the study. Disease activity dropped more in the exercise group, and inflammation, physical function and heart health also improved [[10]](#source-10). The authors concluded that the results debunk concerns that hard exercise worsens the disease [[10]](#source-10).

## What a physical therapy program can include

A program for ankylosing spondylitis usually aims at the things the disease tends to take away:

- **Mobility:** flexibility and stretching exercises for the spine and hips [[8]](#source-8)
- **Posture:** exercises to improve posture and body position, a key goal of treatment [[1]](#source-1)
- **Breathing:** breathing exercises, since stiffness where the ribs meet the spine can limit deep breaths [[1]](#source-1) [[8]](#source-8)
- **Strength and fitness:** strength and cardio training, which can be progressed to higher intensities [[8]](#source-8) [[10]](#source-10)

Regular exercise at home matters, and in trials, supervised group sessions did somewhat better than home exercise alone [[9]](#source-9). A therapist can teach the exercises, track your mobility over time and adjust the plan as your symptoms change.

## When to see a clinician

**See a doctor** if you have back pain that has lasted several months, especially if it started before 45, is worse with rest and better with movement. A rheumatologist can confirm the diagnosis and discuss medicine [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3).

**Get your eyes checked quickly** if an eye becomes painful, red or sensitive to light, since eye inflammation is common with ankylosing spondylitis and can affect your vision [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2).

**Get checked after a fall** or a new, different back pain if your spine is stiff or fused, because spine fractures are a known complication [[3]](#source-3).

## The takeaway

- Ankylosing spondylitis is inflammatory arthritis of the spine. Its back pain often gets worse with rest and better with movement, and it usually starts before 45.
- The diagnosis is often delayed by years. Back pain with this pattern is worth bringing up with a doctor.
- US guidelines strongly recommend physical therapy, and prefer active, supervised exercise to passive treatments.
- Exercise programs reduced pain and modestly improved function and disease activity compared with no exercise.
- High-intensity exercise did not make the disease worse in a trial. It lowered disease activity and improved fitness.

You can start physical therapy in Utah without a referral. If you have ankylosing spondylitis, or back pain that eases when you move, an evaluation can measure your mobility and posture and build an exercise plan to keep your spine moving, working alongside the care from your rheumatologist.
