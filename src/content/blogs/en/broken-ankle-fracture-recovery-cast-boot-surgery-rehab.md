You stepped off a curb wrong, slipped on ice or came down badly on someone's foot, and the X-ray confirmed it: a broken ankle. Ankle fractures are the fourth most common fracture in adults [[1]](#source-1), and the first questions are almost always the same. How long will this take? Will I need surgery? When can I walk on it?

Treatment has changed in the past few years. Large trials have tested shorter time in a cast, removable boots, casting instead of surgery and walking sooner after an operation. Here is what they found, and what it means for getting back on your feet.

## Which bone, and is the ankle stable?

The ankle joint is formed by the two lower leg bones, the tibia and fibula, sitting on top of the talus bone of the foot. A fracture can involve the outer bony bump (the lateral malleolus, at the end of the fibula), the inner one (the medial malleolus), or both, and sometimes the back of the tibia as well [[1]](#source-1).

What matters most for treatment is whether the ankle joint stays stable. Many fractures of the outer ankle alone are stable enough to bear weight. Others leave the joint unstable, and some of those need surgery [[1]](#source-1). Typical signs of a fracture include immediate, severe pain, swelling, bruising, tenderness, not being able to put weight on the foot and, especially with a dislocation, a visible deformity [[1]](#source-1).

## How long does recovery take?

Most ankle fractures take at least 6 weeks for early healing and 10 to 12 weeks to heal completely, and ligaments and tendons may take longer. Most people return to normal activities within 3 to 4 months, but full recovery from some fractures can take up to 2 years [[1]](#source-1). Stiffness and swelling can last for many months, even after the bone has healed [[1]](#source-1).

Recovery is fast at first and then slows: about 80% of function at 6 months, with little further gain after that and, on average, recovery still incomplete at 2 years [[3]](#source-3). Studies with older participants tended to report worse results [[3]](#source-3). If you have right ankle surgery, driving may resume at about 9 to 12 weeks [[1]](#source-1).

## Less time in a cast, and boots instead of casts

For decades, 6 weeks in a cast was standard. Trials now show that, for the right fracture, less can be enough:

| Trial | Who took part | What was compared | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kortekangas, 2019 [[4]](#source-4) | 247 people with a stable fracture of the outer ankle | 6 weeks in a cast vs. 3 weeks in a cast vs. 3 weeks in a simple brace | 3 weeks was as good as 6 at 1 year |
| Kearney, 2021 [[5]](#source-5) | 669 adults with a fracture suitable for a cast | Plaster cast vs. removable brace with ankle exercises from the start | No difference in ankle function at 16 weeks |
| SUPER-FIN, 2026 [[6]](#source-6) | 126 people with an outer ankle fracture that was unstable on a stress test but well aligned | Cast for 6 weeks vs. surgery plus a cast | Cast as good as surgery at 2 years, with fewer complications |
| AIM, 2016 [[7]](#source-7) | 620 adults over 60 with an unstable fracture | A closely molded cast vs. surgery | Similar ankle function at 6 months |

In the first trial, 1-year ankle scores were 87.6 after 6 weeks in a cast, 91.7 after 3 weeks in a cast and 89.8 after 3 weeks in a brace. Both 3-week options met the test set in advance for being no worse than 6 weeks [[4]](#source-4).

In the SUPER-FIN trial, ankle scores at 2 years were 89 with a cast and 87 with surgery. In the surgery group, 9 people needed another procedure to remove the hardware, and two of them developed infections [[6]](#source-6).

## Surgery or a cast for older adults

Surgery carries extra risks, and they rise with smoking, diabetes and older age [[1]](#source-1). The AIM trial tested whether older adults with an unstable fracture could avoid an operation. Its 620 participants averaged 71 years old, and 74% were women [[7]](#source-7):

- **Ankle function at 6 months** was equivalent with a close contact cast and with surgery [[7]](#source-7).
- **Infection and wound problems** were more common after surgery (10% vs. 1%) [[7]](#source-7).
- **The bone healed out of position** more often with a cast (15% vs. 3%) [[7]](#source-7).
- **19% of those who started with a cast** later had surgery because the fracture slipped out of position, which was allowed in the trial [[7]](#source-7).

So for an older adult, a well-applied cast is a reasonable option to discuss, with the understanding that some will still need an operation.

## Walking sooner after surgery

After surgery, people were traditionally told to keep weight off the ankle for 6 weeks. The WAX trial randomly assigned 561 adults who had surgery for an unstable fracture to start walking on it at 2 weeks or at 6 weeks [[8]](#source-8). At 4 months, ankle scores were slightly better with early walking (65.9 vs. 61.2), complications were similar (16% vs. 14%) and costs were no higher [[8]](#source-8).

A 2024 Cochrane review of 53 studies and 4,489 adults agreed that early weight-bearing after surgery probably leads to slightly better ankle function, though the difference may be too small to notice. A removable support after surgery may also help a little, and neither approach appears to increase the need for another operation [[9]](#source-9).

## Does a rehab exercise program help?

This is where the evidence is most surprising. In the EXACT trial, 214 adults with an isolated, uncomplicated ankle fracture were randomly assigned, on the day their cast or boot came off, to a supervised exercise program plus advice or to advice alone, both from a physical therapist [[10]](#source-10). At 3 months, both groups had improved by the same amount, and the supervised program added no benefit for activity or quality of life [[10]](#source-10).

The Cochrane review was also uncertain whether any specific physical therapy approach beat usual care, mostly because the studies were small and different from one another [[9]](#source-9).

That does not mean rehab does not matter. OrthoInfo describes rehabilitation as central to recovery whether or not you had surgery [[1]](#source-1), and in EXACT both groups got guidance from a physical therapist [[10]](#source-10). What it suggests is that for a simple fracture, a clear home program and good advice may be enough, while people who are not progressing, had surgery, or have more complex injuries may need more hands-on help.

## What you can do now

- **While in a cast or boot,** rest and raise your ankle when you can, and gently move your toes and bend your knee to keep the muscles from stiffening [[2]](#source-2).
- **Follow the weight-bearing plan** your surgeon gives you, and ask whether early walking is an option for your fracture [[8]](#source-8) [[9]](#source-9).
- **Ask about a shorter time in a cast or a removable brace** if your fracture is stable [[4]](#source-4) [[5]](#source-5).
- **Once the cast is off,** follow an exercise program to restore motion and strength, and build up walking gradually [[1]](#source-1).
- **Expect some swelling and stiffness** for months, and do not judge your recovery by the first 6 weeks [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3).

## When to get medical help

**Call 911 or go to the emergency room** after an ankle injury if your ankle is at an odd angle, a bone is sticking out, you have a bad cut or wound on the ankle, you are in severe pain, or your toes look blue or white or feel numb. Do not drive yourself [[2]](#source-2).

**Get urgent medical advice** if the ankle is very painful or the pain is getting worse, swelling or bruising is large or increasing, it hurts to put weight on it, or it feels very stiff or hard to move [[2]](#source-2).

**Contact your surgeon or clinician** if you notice signs of infection after surgery, or if your recovery seems to stall well beyond the usual 3 to 4 months [[1]](#source-1).

## The takeaway

- A broken ankle usually needs 6 to 12 weeks to heal, and most people return to normal activities in 3 to 4 months, though full recovery can take longer.
- For a stable fracture, 3 weeks in a cast or brace worked as well as 6 weeks in a cast, and a removable brace worked as well as a plaster cast.
- For some fractures, including in older adults, a cast gave results similar to surgery with fewer infections.
- After surgery, walking on the ankle at 2 weeks was at least as good as waiting 6 weeks.
- For a simple fracture, good advice and a home program may be enough; more hands-on rehab makes sense if you are not progressing.

You can start physical therapy in Utah without a referral. Once your cast or boot comes off, an evaluation can check your motion, strength and balance and set up a plan that matches your fracture and your goals, whether that is a home program or more hands-on help.
