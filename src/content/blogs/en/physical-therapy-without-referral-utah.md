**Short answer: no.** In Utah you can make an appointment with a licensed physical therapist directly, without first getting a referral from a doctor. Your insurance plan may have its own rules, though, so it is worth knowing how both pieces fit together.

## What Utah law says

Utah Code section 58-24b-307 is titled "No referral required for physical therapy." It reads [[1]](#source-1):

> "A physical therapist may evaluate, initiate, and provide physical therapy treatment for a client without a referral from another health service provider."

That wording was added in 2025 by H.B. 188, which "clarifies that no referral is needed for physical therapy or occupational therapy" [[2]](#source-2). The key word is *clarifies*: Utah patients have had direct access to physical therapists since 1985, and the American Physical Therapy Association (APTA) lists Utah among the states with **unrestricted** direct access, meaning no time or visit limits [[4]](#source-4).

Utah law also sets clear boundaries on what physical therapy includes. Physical therapists in Utah do not diagnose diseases, prescribe drugs, perform acupuncture or surgery, or take X-rays [[5]](#source-5). If your evaluation turns up signs of something outside that scope, a good physical therapist will send you to the right provider.

## How Utah compares with other states

Every state now allows some form of direct access, but many still add restrictions [[3]](#source-3):

| Type of direct access (APTA, July 2025) | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Unrestricted: evaluation and treatment with no limits | 21 states, including Utah |
| Provisional: limits on time or visits, or a referral required for certain procedures | 29 states, the District of Columbia and the US Virgin Islands |

*Sources: APTA, State of Direct Access to Physical Therapist Services, July 2025 [[3]](#source-3); Utah’s category from APTA’s state-by-state summary [[4]](#source-4).*

## What about insurance?

State law decides whether a physical therapist may treat you without a referral. Your health plan decides what it will pay for. These are separate questions.

- **Medicare.** Medicare does not require a doctor’s order for outpatient physical therapy. It does require that a physician or other qualified provider **certify your physical therapy plan of care**, and payment depends on that certification. Your therapist sends the plan to your doctor, who should certify it as soon as possible and within 30 days of your first treatment [[6]](#source-6).
- **Private insurance, Medicare Advantage and Medicaid plans.** Some plans require a referral or prior authorization even when state law does not, and some health systems have their own referral policies [[3]](#source-3). Before your first visit, check your plan, or ask us to check it with you.
- **After a car accident.** Care is often billed to auto insurance (personal injury protection) instead of health insurance, which has its own rules.

## Does skipping the referral affect your care?

The research so far is encouraging, with the caveat that none of these studies are randomized trials, so they show associations rather than proof:

- In claims data from more than 62,000 physical therapy episodes, people who self-referred had **fewer visits** (86% as many) and **lower costs** ($0.87 for every $1.00), with **no difference** in related health care use afterward [[7]](#source-7).
- In a study of 603 patients with neck or back pain, those who went straight to physical therapy had **lower total costs** (an average of $1,543 less) than those who went through a physician referral, and **both groups improved similarly** in pain and disability [[8]](#source-8).
- A systematic review of 8 studies found that direct-access patients had outcomes and satisfaction that were as good or better, fewer visits, less imaging and fewer medications, and "no evidence for harm" [[9]](#source-9).

## The takeaway

- In Utah, you do not need a referral to be evaluated and treated by a physical therapist.
- Your insurance plan may still have its own referral or authorization rules, so check before your first visit.
- Medicare covers outpatient physical therapy without a referral, as long as a doctor certifies the plan of care.
- Studies of direct access show fewer visits and lower costs, with no evidence of harm.
