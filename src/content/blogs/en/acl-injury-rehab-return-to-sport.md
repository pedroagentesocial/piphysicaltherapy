The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) keeps the shin bone from sliding and twisting under the thigh bone. It is often torn during a sudden stop, cut or awkward landing. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons estimates about **200,000 ACL injuries a year in the United States** [[1]](#source-1). Female athletes are at higher risk: in high school sports, girls had about **1.6 times** the risk of boys per exposure, and girls’ soccer had the highest risk per season [[2]](#source-2).

If you or your child has just torn an ACL, here is what the research says about the road back.

## Surgery first, or rehab first?

For young, active adults, two well-known trials tested starting with structured rehabilitation and having surgery later only if the knee stayed unstable.

- In the Swedish KANON trial, people who had rehab with optional later surgery did **as well at two years** as those who had early reconstruction [[3]](#source-3). At **five years** there was still no meaningful difference in knee scores, activity level or knee osteoarthritis on X-rays [[4]](#source-4).
- In the Dutch COMPARE trial, early surgery scored slightly higher at two years (84.7 vs. 79.4 out of 100), a difference the authors described as not clinically relevant [[5]](#source-5).

About half the people in the rehab-first groups eventually chose surgery:

| Trial and follow-up | Rehab-first patients who later had reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| KANON, 2 years | 23 of 59 (39%) |
| KANON, 5 years | 30 of 59 (51%) |
| COMPARE, 2 years | 41 of 82 (50%) |

*Sources: Frobell et al., 2010 and 2013 [[3]](#source-3) [[4]](#source-4); Reijman et al., 2021 [[5]](#source-5).*

That means rehab first is a reasonable option for some people, and for others surgery is the right call. The orthopedic guideline notes that when surgery is chosen for an isolated ACL tear, **early reconstruction is preferred**, because the risk of further cartilage and meniscus damage starts to rise within three months [[1]](#source-1). This is a decision to make with an orthopedic surgeon, based on your knee, your sport and your goals. Either way, rehabilitation is the backbone of recovery.

## What rehab after ACL surgery involves

The physical therapy guideline for knee ligament injuries recommends [[6]](#source-6):

- **Moving early**, within the first week after surgery
- **Supervised rehab in the clinic plus a home program**, with education so you can do it correctly on your own
- **Strength training** for the thigh muscles, starting within 4 to 6 weeks and continuing **2 to 3 times a week for 6 to 10 months**
- **Neuromuscular electrical stimulation** for 6 to 8 weeks alongside strengthening
- **Neuromuscular training**: balance, landing and movement-control work
- **Hop tests** to help decide when you are ready to return to activities, and a questionnaire to check psychological readiness, since fear of reinjury can hold people back [[6]](#source-6)

A 2023 international guideline adds that a visit **before surgery** may help, with possible gains in quadriceps strength, knee motion and time to return, though the certainty of that evidence is low [[7]](#source-7). It also calls exercise "the mainstay" of rehab and says the length of rehab should be based on meeting criteria, not just on time [[7]](#source-7).

People who returned to their pre-injury level were more likely to have symmetrical hop test results, to be younger, and to have a positive psychological response to recovery [[8]](#source-8).

## When is it safe to go back?

Returning too early is one of the biggest risks. In the Delaware-Oslo cohort, 100 athletes in pivoting sports were followed for two years after ACL reconstruction [[9]](#source-9):

  <ul>
    <li style="--v:99"><span>Returned before 9 months</span><span>39.5%</span></li>
    <li style="--v:49"><span>Returned after 9 months</span><span>19.4%</span></li>
  </ul>
  <p class="chart-source">Source: Grindem et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2016 <a href="#source-9">[9]</a>. Bars are scaled to 40%.</p>
</figure>

The reinjury rate dropped by **51% for each month** that return was delayed, up to 9 months after surgery [[9]](#source-9). Strength mattered too: each 1-point improvement in quadriceps strength symmetry was linked to a 3% lower reinjury rate [[9]](#source-9).

The study also tested a set of criteria: scoring above 90% on quadriceps strength symmetry, four single-leg hop tests, a knee function questionnaire and the athlete’s own rating of knee function. Only 1 of the 18 athletes who passed was reinjured, compared with 21 of 55 who did not pass [[9]](#source-9). The study was small, so that difference was not statistically significant, but together with the timing finding it supports a simple message: **wait at least 9 months, and pass objective tests before going back** [[9]](#source-9).

This matters because second ACL injuries are common. Across 19 studies, **15%** of people had a second ACL injury (to either knee), and among athletes under 25 who returned to sport the rate was **23%, nearly 1 in 4** [[10]](#source-10).

## Preventing ACL injuries

The good news is that ACL injuries can often be prevented. The physical therapy prevention guideline gives its highest grade of recommendation to **exercise-based knee injury prevention programs**, such as the FIFA 11+ and similar warm-ups, for athletes 12 to 25 years old in high-risk sports, and especially for young female athletes [[11]](#source-11). It recommends using them with all young athletes, not just those screened as high risk [[11]](#source-11).

A review that combined eight meta-analyses found these programs cut ACL injuries by about **50%** overall, and non-contact ACL injuries in female athletes by about **67%** [[12]](#source-12). After reconstruction, the same kind of neuromuscular training in the late phase of rehab may also help prevent a second injury [[11]](#source-11).

## The takeaway

- Rehabilitation is the core of ACL recovery, with or without surgery.
- Rehab first with optional later surgery is a reasonable option for some young adults, but about half eventually choose reconstruction.
- After surgery, plan on months of progressive strength and neuromuscular training.
- Waiting at least 9 months and passing strength and hop tests before returning is linked to a much lower reinjury rate.
- Prevention warm-ups can cut ACL injury risk roughly in half.
