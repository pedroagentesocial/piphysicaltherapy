A fall off a mountain bike. A tackle on the field. A slip on the ice that lands you right on your shoulder. The collarbone is one of the bones people break most often, and the pain, the sagging shoulder and the bump over the bone can be alarming.

The good news is that most broken collarbones heal well, and most heal without surgery [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2). The harder question is what to do when the two ends of the bone have shifted apart. Several good trials have tested a sling against surgery, and their results are more nuanced than "surgery is better." Here is what the evidence shows.

## What a broken collarbone is

The collarbone, or clavicle, is the long, thin bone that runs across the top of the chest and connects the arm to the body. Most breaks happen from a direct blow to the shoulder, such as a fall onto it, a car crash or a sports collision. A fall onto an outstretched arm can also do it [[1]](#source-1).

Common signs include [[1]](#source-1):

- Pain, and trouble moving or lifting the arm
- A shoulder that sags downward and forward
- A grinding feeling when you try to raise the arm
- A bump or change in shape over the break
- Bruising, swelling or tenderness along the collarbone

Clavicle fractures make up somewhere between about 2.6% and 5% of all fractures, depending on the study [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3). In an Italian hospital study of 535 clavicle fractures, 68% were in men and 81% were in the middle third of the bone. About half of those middle breaks were displaced, meaning the ends had moved out of line [[3]](#source-3). That middle-third, displaced break is where the sling-or-surgery debate happens.

## When to get care fast

**Go to the emergency room** if the shoulder has changed shape or swollen quickly, if bone is poking through the skin, or if you have heavy bleeding, trouble breathing, chest pain or are coughing up blood [[2]](#source-2). Those signs can mean a more serious injury.

Otherwise, see a doctor soon after the injury if your shoulder is very painful, swollen or bruised, or you cannot use your arm normally. An X-ray confirms the break and shows how far the ends have moved [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2).

## Most breaks heal in a sling

When the bone ends have not shifted much, the usual treatment is a simple arm sling for comfort, pain relief and follow-up X-rays to make sure the bone is healing in a good position [[1]](#source-1). The NHS says the sling is often needed for 2 to 3 weeks, followed by a few more weeks of resting the shoulder, and that most adults recover in 6 to 8 weeks, while children usually take 3 to 4 weeks [[2]](#source-2). OrthoInfo notes that full healing of the bone can take several months, whether or not you have surgery, and that it may take longer in people who smoke or have diabetes [[1]](#source-1).

What about the figure-of-eight brace that wraps around both shoulders? In a Danish trial of 79 people, a simple sling caused less discomfort than a figure-of-eight bandage, with the same function and appearance after healing [[10]](#source-10). A Cochrane review found the evidence comparing the two too weak to say that either one is best [[11]](#source-11). For most people, a sling is the simpler and more comfortable choice.

## Sling or surgery for a displaced break

The common operation uses a metal plate and screws to hold the bone in place while it heals. Pins or screws may be used instead [[1]](#source-1). Here is how the main trials compare:

| Study | What it found |
| --- | --- |
| Canadian trial, 132 people (2007) | Surgery gave better shoulder scores, faster healing and fewer nonunions at 1 year. Most problems after surgery were related to the hardware [[5]](#source-5) |
| UK trial, 200 people (2013) | 1 nonunion after surgery vs. 16 with a sling. Once people whose bone did not heal were excluded, function was the same [[6]](#source-6) |
| Dutch trial, 160 people (2017) | Far fewer nonunions with surgery, but no difference in shoulder function, and more second operations [[7]](#source-7) |
| UK trial, 301 people (2017) | Fewer nonunions at 9 months and better early function with surgery [[8]](#source-8) |
| Cochrane review, 14 trials, 1,469 people (2019) | Surgery may not improve function, pain or quality of life, but may lower the chance of a second operation for a problem [[4]](#source-4) |

The pattern is fairly consistent. Surgery makes it much more likely that the bone will heal, and it may help you get your shoulder back a bit faster in the first months [[5]](#source-5) [[8]](#source-8). But by a year, most people treated in a sling end up with similar function, and surgery brings its own problems [[4]](#source-4) [[7]](#source-7).

Many of the second operations in the plate group were planned removals of the plate, and 19% of people who had surgery were left with numbness around the scar [[7]](#source-7). Across the trials in the Cochrane review, removal of hardware because of discomfort happened in about 10% of people after surgery and infection in about 3%. Healing out of position with symptoms was more common with a sling, at about 11% versus 1% [[4]](#source-4).

In other words, neither choice is free of risk. The Cochrane authors conclude that the decision should be made one person at a time, weighing the benefits and harms of each option and the person's own preferences [[4]](#source-4).

## Who is more likely to need surgery

A bone that does not heal, called a nonunion, does not always cause trouble, but it can lead to pain and weakness that need surgery later [[1]](#source-1). In a Scottish study that followed 941 broken collarbone shafts treated without surgery, 13% of displaced breaks in adults did not heal. Smoking was the strongest risk factor, raising the odds almost fourfold, followed by a break in several pieces and how far the ends had moved apart [[9]](#source-9). The authors stress that quitting smoking should be part of treatment [[9]](#source-9).

Doctors generally consider surgery when the ends are very displaced [[1]](#source-1). Other things that often come up in the conversation are your job, your sport, how soon you need full use of the arm and how you feel about a bump versus a scar.

## Rehab: how to get your shoulder back

Whether you have a sling or surgery, the goal of rehab is the same: get the shoulder moving again without stressing the bone before it is ready. OrthoInfo describes the usual steps [[1]](#source-1):

1. **Right away:** keep the hand, wrist and elbow moving so they do not stiffen while the arm rests in the sling.
2. **Once the bone starts to heal:** begin gentle shoulder motion exercises.
3. **As healing progresses:** add strengthening, step by step.

You can follow a home plan or work with a therapist, and sticking with the program matters for getting back to the activities you enjoy [[1]](#source-1). The NHS also advises doing your exercises regularly and keeping up your daily routine as much as you can without straining the shoulder [[2]](#source-2).

A physical therapist can help you judge when to move from one stage to the next, restore the reach you need for things like dressing and washing your hair, and rebuild strength in the muscles around the shoulder and shoulder blade.

## Getting back to work, sport and lifting

Most people return to their regular activities within 3 months, once their doctor confirms that the bone is stable [[1]](#source-1). Do not lift heavy things or play sport while the bone is healing [[2]](#source-2). Lifting or returning to activity too early can make the pieces shift or the hardware break, which may mean starting treatment over [[1]](#source-1). Sports and physical activity are safe once the fracture has fully healed [[1]](#source-1).

## The takeaway

- Most broken collarbones heal well in a simple sling, usually with recovery in 6 to 8 weeks for adults and full bone healing over a few months.
- Go to the emergency room for bone through the skin, a shoulder that has changed shape, trouble breathing or chest pain.
- For displaced breaks, surgery makes healing much more likely and may speed early recovery, but by a year function is usually similar, and surgery has its own risks and second operations.
- Smoking raises the chance that the bone will not heal. Quitting helps.
- Rehab moves in stages: keep the elbow, wrist and hand moving early, add gentle shoulder motion as the bone heals, then rebuild strength.

You can start physical therapy in Utah without a referral. After a broken collarbone, once your doctor confirms the bone is healing, an evaluation can check your shoulder's motion and strength and build a plan to get you back to work, sport and everyday tasks.
