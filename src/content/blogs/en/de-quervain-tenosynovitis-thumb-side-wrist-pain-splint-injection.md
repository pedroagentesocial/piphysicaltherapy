It hurts on the thumb side of your wrist every time you lift your baby, pour a pot of coffee or turn a key. There may be a little swelling just above the base of the thumb, and making a fist with your thumb tucked inside sends a sharp pain up the side of your wrist.

That is the typical picture of de Quervain's tenosynovitis, sometimes called "mommy thumb." It happens when two thumb tendons stop gliding smoothly through the narrow tunnel they share at the wrist [[1]](#source-1). It is a different problem from thumb arthritis, which causes pain deeper at the base of the thumb. The good news is that most people get better without surgery. Here is what the research shows about who gets it and what helps.

## What it is

Two tendons that move the thumb, the abductor pollicis longus and the extensor pollicis brevis, run together through a tunnel of tissue and bone on the thumb side of the wrist. In de Quervain's, their gliding through that tunnel is resisted, which causes pain [[1]](#source-1). Pain tends to show up when you grip, pinch, lift with the thumb pointed up or bend the wrist toward the little finger.

The diagnosis is usually made from your history and a hands-on exam. A common test is to tuck the thumb into the palm, close the fingers around it and gently bend the wrist toward the little finger. A sharp pain along the thumb side of the wrist points to de Quervain's.

## Who gets it

In a study of 9,696 randomly selected working-age adults in England, de Quervain's was found in 0.5% of men and 1.3% of women [[2]](#source-2). A French study that examined 3,710 workers found it in 0.6% of men and 2.1% of women [[4]](#source-4).

A study of U.S. military personnel counted 11,332 cases over 9 years. The rate was 2.8 cases per 1,000 person-years in women and 0.6 in men. People over 40 had a higher rate than those under 20, and the rate was also higher in Black service members than in white service members [[3]](#source-3).

In the French workers, the risk was higher with age and in women. Work factors linked with it included repeatedly or constantly bending the wrist to extreme positions and repeated twisting or screwing motions [[4]](#source-4).

### During pregnancy and after the baby arrives

De Quervain's is common during pregnancy and breastfeeding, and in that group it tends to settle on its own [[5]](#source-5). In a small randomized study of 18 women who were pregnant or breastfeeding, all 9 who received a cortisone injection had complete pain relief, with one later recurrence. None of those who wore a thumb splint had complete relief. But at the end of the breastfeeding period, 8 of the splint group had recovered without other treatment [[5]](#source-5).

## What helps

### Instructions and changing how you use the hand

A European panel of 35 hand therapists, hand surgeons and rehabilitation physicians agreed that everyone with de Quervain's should get instructions on how to use the hand and lower the strain on the tendons. They also agreed that instructions alone are not enough and should be combined with another treatment, such as a splint, anti-inflammatory medicine, an injection or, in some cases, surgery [[1]](#source-1). The choice depends on how severe the problem is, how long it has lasted and what has already been tried [[1]](#source-1).

### Splints and injections

A thumb splint (called a thumb spica) rests the tendons by holding the thumb and wrist still. A corticosteroid injection into the tendon tunnel calms the irritation.

Those studies were descriptive and did not compare groups directly, so the numbers should be read with caution [[6]](#source-6). A later meta-analysis of two randomized trials found that steroid injection worked better than splinting, with about 1 in every 2 people treated benefiting from the injection [[7]](#source-7).

Adding a splint to the injection may help, though. In a randomized trial of 67 patients, treatment success was 93% when the injection was combined with a thumb cast and 69% with the injection alone [[8]](#source-8). A 2018 systematic review found moderate evidence that corticosteroid injections help in the very short term, and moderate evidence that adding a thumb splint to an injection helps in the short and medium term [[9]](#source-9).

### Surgery

When the pain does not improve with other treatment, a hand surgeon can release the tunnel so the tendons glide freely. The European panel lists surgery among the suitable options, chosen mainly by how severe and long-lasting the problem is [[1]](#source-1).

## What recovery usually looks like

Every case is different, but care built on this evidence usually:

- **Starts with the right diagnosis.** Pain on the thumb side of the wrist can also come from thumb arthritis, a wrist sprain or a nerve problem.
- **Lowers the strain.** Changing how you lift, grip and twist, and using a splint to rest the tendons [[1]](#source-1) [[9]](#source-9).
- **Adds an injection when needed.** Your doctor may recommend one, often with a splint afterward [[7]](#source-7) [[8]](#source-8).
- **Brings strength and use back gradually.** Once the pain settles, the hand and wrist are eased back into full use.

For new parents, symptoms often fade once breastfeeding ends, but an injection can bring faster relief if the pain is hard to live with [[5]](#source-5).

## What a physical therapist adds

A physical therapist can:

- Examine the wrist and thumb and check for other causes of pain in the area
- Teach you how to lift, grip and carry with less strain on the tendons, including how to pick up and hold a baby [[1]](#source-1) [[4]](#source-4)
- Fit or recommend a thumb splint and explain when to wear it [[8]](#source-8) [[9]](#source-9)
- Look at your work or sports tasks that involve wrist bending and twisting [[4]](#source-4)
- Guide a gradual return to full use of the hand once the pain calms down
- Coordinate with your doctor if an injection or a surgical opinion makes sense [[1]](#source-1) [[7]](#source-7)

## The takeaway

- De Quervain's causes pain on the thumb side of the wrist when two thumb tendons stop gliding smoothly.
- It is more common in women, in people over 40 and in jobs with repeated wrist bending and twisting, and it often appears during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Everyone should get instructions on using the hand with less strain, combined with another treatment.
- Steroid injections work better than a splint alone, and adding a splint to an injection may improve results.
- Most people improve without surgery. Surgery is an option when other treatment fails.

You can start physical therapy in Utah without a referral. If thumb-side wrist pain is making it hard to lift your child, work or grip, an evaluation can confirm what is going on and build a plan to calm it down.
